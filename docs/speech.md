## 1. The climate has shifted (0:30, clock 0:30)

*(click)* The climate has shifted. *(click)* Farm plans haven't.

*(click)* This is the Karshi oasis in Uzbekistan, seen from space by NASA's Terra satellite.
Every spring, farmers here decide what to plant, one year at a time, by habit.
We are team KarPS, and this is DalaShift. It doesn't just pick the most profitable crop for this year.
It picks the best five-year future for the field.

## 2. The problem (0:45, clock 1:15)

Three facts.
*(click)* About seventy percent of the world's freshwater goes to agriculture, according to FAO. Every wasted cubic metre matters.
*(click)* Heat and drought are changing when and where crops can grow, but most farmers still follow old calendars.
*(click)* And crops are chosen one season at a time. Nobody shows the next five years, so damage to soil and water shows up too late.

*(click)* We tested this in Uzbekistan: hot, dry and fully irrigated. In Karshi, NASA POWER shows very hot days rose from 96 to 104 a year.
And NASA SMAP shows that without canal water, the soil dries 41 percent from spring to summer.

## 3. One year at a time (0:35, clock 1:50)

Here is the usual plan: wheat, then cotton, then wheat again.
This farm gets 8,000 cubic metres of water per hectare a year. That's the line on the chart.
Wheat fits easily. But every cotton year needs almost 13,000. That's 62 percent more water than the farm has.
So in every cotton year the farm is either short of water, or it takes water from someone else.
The plan isn't wrong for one year. It's wrong over five.

## 4. DalaShift plans five years ahead (0:45, clock 2:35)

This is DalaShift on a phone, in Uzbek, because that's the language our farmers read.
*(click)* Step one: pick your field. Choose a district, or press "My location".
*(click)* Step two: see what changed. Three plain facts from NASA data: more hot days, less rain, drier soil.
*(click)* Step three: get a five-year plan. Set your water and your goal (income, balanced, or water saving), and say whether cotton or wheat is required.

## 5. NASA satellites, explained like a neighbour (0:40, clock 3:15)

We use three NASA sources.
NASA POWER: 35 years of daily weather, for heat, frost, rain and how much water each crop needs.
SMAP: how dry the soil is under the field. HLS, from Landsat and Sentinel-2: how green each field is.
But a farmer doesn't need the word "evapotranspiration". So we translate.
*(click)* "8 more very hot days a year than in the 1990s."
*(click)* "14 millimetres less rain in the growing season."
*(click)* "Each hectare needs about 370 more cubic metres of water a year."

## 6. The last five years, replayed (0:45, clock 4:00)

Does it work? We replayed the last five years on the same field, with real NASA weather from 2021 to 2025.
On the left, the usual wheat-cotton plan. It was short of water in 2 of 5 years, by 8,700 cubic metres in total.
It had no legume years, and its rotation health score is 5 out of 10.
On the right, DalaShift's balanced plan: chickpea, wheat, chickpea, wheat, barley.
It fits the water every single year. Two chickpea years put nitrogen back into the soil, and its health score is 8 out of 10.
And the estimated income goes up, from about 26 and a half to 32 million so'm per hectare over five years.
Less water, healthier soil, more income.

### Notes for the speaker
- If you're running late, cut the Slide 5 quotes to two ("8 more hot days" and "41 percent drier soil"). That saves about 10 seconds.
- Likely questions:
  - **Is the income real?** It's an estimate: we use the low end of a ±15% range, and costs are placeholders until we have real farm data.
  - **Is the weather data exact for my field?** NASA POWER has a resolution of about 50 km, so it's right for the region, and SMAP and HLS add detail for each field.
  - **Do you use AI/ML?** No. The planner checks every plan with clear rules and FAO-56 water maths, so every result can be explained.
