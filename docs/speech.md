## Slide 2: The problem (0:45, clock 1:15)

**Speaker 1:**
The problem: farms are planning for a climate that no longer exists.

[click] Seventy percent. About seventy percent of the world's freshwater goes to farming, says FAO AQUASTAT. Every wasted cubic metre counts.

[click] Shifting. Heat and drought are changing when and where crops can grow, but farmers still plan by habit and old calendars.

[click] One season. Crops are chosen one year at a time. Nobody shows the next five, so soil and water damage appears too late.

[click] Our test case is Uzbekistan. In Karshi, NASA POWER shows very hot days rose from 96 to 104 a year. NASA SMAP shows the soil, without canal water, dries 41 percent from spring to summer.

## Slide 3: But fields are still planned one year at a time. (0:35, clock 1:50)

**Speaker 2:**
This is the usual plan in Uzbekistan: wheat, cotton, wheat, cotton, wheat.

Each bar is the irrigation water that year's crop needs. The line across the chart is what this farm actually gets: 8,000 cubic metres per hectare a year.

The green wheat years need about 3,600, well under the line. The red cotton years need almost 13,000.

So cotton years need 62 percent more water than the farm gets. For one year, the plan works. Over five, it doesn't.

### Notes for the speakers
- Hand over between speakers at the slide change, without saying "now my colleague will...". That saves time.
- If you're running late: on slide 5, read only two quotes (hot days and soil), which saves about 10 seconds.
- Likely questions:
  - **Is the income real?** It's an estimate: we use the cautious low end of a ±15% range, and costs are placeholders until we have real farm data.
  - **Is the weather exact for my field?** NASA POWER is about 50 km resolution, so it's right for the region, and SMAP and HLS add detail for each field.
  - **Do you use AI/ML?** No. The planner checks every possible plan with clear rules and FAO-56 water maths, so every result can be explained.
